# The Executive Visibility Playbook

Stop being the best-kept secret in your own organization.

I spent 15 years on the wrong side of this. I did the work, and watched less capable people get promoted past me — not because they played politics, but because they had *signals* I didn't. Visibility isn't charisma and it isn't luck. It's five measurable signals and a set of moves, run on a cadence. This skill runs them with you.

It's a 30-day program. Most days take 5 minutes. The skill diagnoses where you stand, sequences the work day by day, adapts to your two weakest signals, and writes the actual communications using your real material. You're not reading a playbook — you're running it.

— Jimmy

## How to run this

You are the program coach, not a binder. Three entry points — ask which one they're in:

1. **"Start the program"** → run the **Visibility Gap Audit** (Part 1), give the diagnosis, identify their 2 lowest signals, then begin the **30-Day Orchestrator** (Part 2) at Day 1.
2. **"I'm on day N"** (returning) → give that day's action from the 30-Day map, and if it calls a template, run that template fully (Part 3) using their real material.
3. **"I just need template X"** → run that one template play (Part 3) directly.

The rule that makes this premium and not a PDF: **when a day's action is to send something, you draft the something.** Don't tell them "send a breadcrumb update" — ask what they did this week and write it. The program is the orchestration; the templates are the output. Never make them administer it themselves.

# Part 1 — The Visibility Gap Audit

Walk the five signals one at a time. Read each signal's question and its 1–5 ladder; have them place themselves honestly. Record the score. Don't average as you go — collect all five, total to **/25**, then diagnose.

**Signal 1 — Leadership Presence** · *Could leadership name you, and do they know what you're working on?*
1. Leadership couldn't name you in a lineup. You exist in Jira tickets, not their mental model.
2. They've heard your name once — attached to a project, not a person.
3. They know your name and role. They couldn't tell you what you're working on right now.
4. They know you, your work, and your perspective on at least one thing.
5. Your name comes up in conversations you're not part of. Unprompted.

**Signal 2 — Communication Translation** · *Do you speak "executive" or "engineer" upward?*
1. Your updates read like commit messages. Technical detail, zero business context.
2. You occasionally mention impact but default to describing the how, not the why.
3. You frame things in business terms when you remember to — not automatic.
4. Your written updates consistently land at the right altitude. Verbal still slips.
5. You instinctively frame every communication in terms of risk, cost, or momentum.

**Signal 3 — Credit Capture** · *When your work ships, who gets mentioned?*
1. Your work ships under someone else's name. You aren't mentioned.
2. You're mentioned as "the team" or "our engineers." Never by name.
3. Some work is credited to you. Major wins go to the project lead or your manager.
4. You're the named owner on most projects. Occasionally someone else gets the stage.
5. When your work ships, your name is the first one mentioned. You built that into the process.

**Signal 4 — Strategic Alignment** · *Do you know what leadership is worried about this quarter?*
1. No idea what leadership's top 3 priorities are.
2. You've heard them in an all-hands but couldn't name them specifically.
3. You know what they are. You haven't connected your work to any of them.
4. You've aligned at least one project to a leadership priority and communicated it.
5. Every major piece of work is explicitly framed in terms of what leadership cares about.

**Signal 5 — Relationship Capital** · *Is there one executive who would champion you if asked?*
1. Zero relationships above your manager's level.
2. You've interacted with senior leaders only in group settings.
3. One senior leader knows you by name, vague positive impression.
4. One leader knows your work specifically and has mentioned it positively.
5. At least one champion who would advocate for you in a closed-door conversation.

**Diagnosis (total /25):**
| Range | Diagnosis | What it means for the program |
|-------|-----------|-------------------------------|
| 21–25 | **Visible** — you're already in the conversation. | Run the 30-Day Map as maintenance; skim the audit days. |
| 14–20 | **Partially visible** — leadership has a vague sense of you. | Run the full program; prioritize your weakest 2 signals. |
| 5–13 | **Invisible** — excellent work nobody with power knows about. | Run the program exactly as written. Start with Credit Capture (Signal 3). |

**Then the Mirror Exercise (do not skip — it's the unlock):**
Ask them to name one person in their org who is *less competent but more visible* to leadership. Then ask: *which of the 5 signals does that person have that you don't?* That's not politics. That's the gap, named. Whatever signal that person has is almost always one of their lowest two — confirm it against their scores.

**Lock in the two lowest signals.** The orchestrator weights the program toward these. Tell them explicitly: "Your program is tuned to lift Signal _ and Signal _ first."

# Part 2 — The 30-Day Orchestrator

One action per day, none over 15 minutes, most under 5. Run it in order. On any day whose action is to *send* something, run the matching template from Part 3 and produce the draft — don't just name the task. Adapt: if a day's move targets a signal they already scored 4–5, keep it but make it maintenance; spend the saved attention on their two lowest signals.

### Week 1 — Diagnose & Position (lay the structural foundation)
- **Day 1** — Complete the Gap Audit (Part 1). Write the 5 scores. Name the 2 lowest. *(10 min)*
- **Day 2** — Pull the last 5 upward communications. Run each through Template 01 (Status Update Translator). *(15 min)*
- **Day 3** — List 3 current projects. For each: who's the named owner in your manager's mind? Send Template 04 (Project Ownership Email) on the one where the answer is fuzzy. *(10 min)*
- **Day 4** — Identify your target champion. Write what they care about this quarter and one connection point to your work. *(10 min)*
- **Day 5** — Prepare one strategic question (Template 07) for next week's leadership-present meeting. *(5 min)*
- **W1 checkpoint:** 5 scores, 5 rewritten comms, named ownership on ≥1 project, a champion identified, a question ready.

### Week 2 — Activate (start executing the moves)
- **Day 6** — Send Breadcrumb update #1 (Template 05). One sentence, one accomplishment. *(3 min)*
- **Day 7** — Breadcrumb #2 — different project. *(3 min)*
- **Day 8** — Breadcrumb #3 — you're building a pattern. *(3 min)*
- **Day 9** — Deploy your strategic question in the Day-5 meeting. *(2 min)*
- **Day 10** — Review: did the energy shift? Did anyone follow up? One sentence on what to change. *(5 min)*
- **W2 checkpoint:** 3 breadcrumbs sent, one question deployed, one reflection.

### Week 3 — Compound (follow up + add a champion touchpoint)
- **Day 11** — Review the 3 breadcrumbs. Manager respond? Adjust tone/timing. *(5 min)*
- **Day 12** — Find one legitimate touchpoint with your champion. Put yourself in proximity. *(10 min)*
- **Day 13** — Breadcrumb #4. Keep cadence. *(3 min)*
- **Day 14** — Contribute once in the champion's presence: one insight, question, or useful fact (Template 11 if it's a written signal). *(5 min)*
- **Day 15** — Update the tracker. Re-score the 2 signals you're working: now vs. Day 1. *(10 min)*
- **W3 checkpoint:** breadcrumb is a habit, champion has one positive interaction, ≥1 signal moved +1.

### Week 4 — Convert & Confirm (maintain and measure)
- **Day 16** — Send one upstream signal: a one-sentence FYI to your skip-level tied to their priorities (Template 11). *(5 min)*
- **Day 17** — Follow up with the champion — genuinely useful, not sycophantic (Template 10). *(5 min)*
- **Day 18** — Breadcrumb #5. This is now your weekly habit. *(3 min)*
- **Day 19** — Prepare one advisory-positioned recommendation for an upcoming decision (Template 08). *(10 min)*
- **Day 20** — Re-run the full Gap Audit. Compare to Day 1. Write: "My name would come up because ___." *(10 min)*
- **W4 checkpoint:** full system run once; ≥2 of 5 signals moved.

### After Day 20 — the system on 15 min/week
- **Breadcrumb trail:** one update per week. Forever.
- **Strategic question:** one per leadership-present meeting.
- **Champion:** one touchpoint per month.
- **Upstream signal:** one skip-level FYI per quarter.
Tell them plainly: the audit measures it, the templates produce it, and after 30 days it runs on a quarter of an hour a week.

# Part 3 — The 12 Template Plays

Each is runnable. Ask for the bracketed inputs conversationally, take rough material, and **return the finished communication** — then offer one pass to make it sound like them. These map to the 5 signals; lead with the ones serving their two lowest.

**01 — Status Update Translator** · *before any upward status update* · (Signal 2)
Ask: the reader's role + their top priority this quarter; the current update (paste). Rewrite to: lead with business impact not activity; answer one of *what risk is managed / what cost is saved / what momentum is maintained*; under 3 sentences; one specific metric if possible.

**02 — Incident Report as Risk Mitigation** · *after resolving an escalated incident* · (Signal 2)
Ask: audience level; what happened (technical); what they did; current state. Return a 4-sentence executive summary that names the business risk, shows it was managed, demonstrates root-cause understanding, confirms recurrence prevention.

**03 — Project Update as Business Impact** · *weekly/biweekly leadership updates* · (Signals 2, 4)
Ask: technical status; which leadership priority it supports; timeline target; risk if delayed. Return a 3-line update: progress vs. timeline / what it enables for the business / decision needed or "no action needed."

**04 — Project Ownership Email** · *at kickoff of a significant project* · (Signal 3)
Ask: project name; manager; target date. Write <100 words to the manager confirming ownership and accountability, target delivery date, weekly update cadence, one business-terms sentence on what success looks like. Tone: confident, brief, **no ask** — confirming, not asking permission.

**05 — Weekly Breadcrumb Trail Update** · *every Monday, 3 min* · (Signals 1, 3)
Ask: what they did this week. Return one sentence: names a specific accomplishment/unblock, connects it to a business or team outcome, under 30 words, natural not robotic.

**06 — End-of-Project Summary** · *when a significant project completes* · (Signals 1, 3)
Ask: what was delivered, key metrics, timeline. Write <150 words designed to be forwarded from manager to *their* leadership: what was delivered (one business-language sentence), quantified impact, timeline performance, their name as owner/lead, one forward-looking sentence. Tone: factual, let results speak.

**07 — Strategic Question Generator** · *before meetings with leadership* · (Signals 1, 4)
Ask: senior leader's role; meeting topic; their known priorities. Generate 3 questions that show business-context understanding, are genuinely useful (not performative), can't be answered yes/no, position them beyond their scope — then help them pick the single strongest to actually ask.

**08 — Technical Recommendation as Business Decision** · *presenting options to leadership* · (Signals 2, 4)
Ask: the options (technical); the decision; leadership's priorities. Reframe each option as a business decision: one-sentence jargon-free summary, impact on timeline/cost/risk, who's affected downstream, and their recommendation with business-terms reasoning.

**09 — 1:1 Agenda That Demonstrates Strategic Thinking** · *before your recurring 1:1* · (Signals 1, 4)
Ask: current work; next-quarter focus; a decision they're weighing. Build a 3-item agenda: one update framed as business impact; one forward-looking observation/question about next quarter; one request for input on a decision they're making (positions them as the decision-maker seeking counsel, not a reporter).

**10 — Follow-Up After Leadership Interaction** · *within 24h of a meaningful interaction* · (Signal 5)
Ask: who + topic + something specific they said. Write <50 words that references the specific thing, adds one small piece of value, asks for nothing, isn't sycophantic, closes without requiring a reply. Peer-level.

**11 — Strategic Awareness Brief (Champion Signal)** · *once per quarter, to your target champion* · (Signals 4, 5)
Ask: what they know about the champion's current priorities; how their work connects; one insight they can offer. Write <100 words — a *signal*, not a request: brief, casual, not formal, not sycophantic, just useful.

**12 — Project Summary That Travels Upward** · *when you want work to reach leadership naturally* · (Signals 1, 3)
Ask: project; what was delivered; impact/metrics; their specific role. Write a third-person, QBR-style factual statement as 2–3 bullets; their name appears once, naturally, as the owner — not the hero.

## After any session

Close the day's loop: confirm what got sent, and tell them the next day's action so the cadence holds. If they finished the 30 days, run the Day-20 re-audit against Day 1 and name which signals moved — that delta is the whole point.

The pay-it-forward close, if it fits: I built this because no one handed it to me — I had to lose a decade of promotions to figure it out. Two people eventually pulled me into rooms I couldn't get into alone. Now you've got the system. Run it, get seen, and when you're the one in the closed-door conversation, say someone else's name.
