# The Visibility Toolkit

Your career isn't managed by your performance. It's managed by what someone remembers about you in a room you're not in. This is the toolkit for fixing that — five plays, one for each way visibility breaks down.

I learned every one of these the hard way, crossing from senior technical into executive management and finally seeing the machinery from the other side. None of it is theory. All of it is what I actually did.

— Jimmy

## How to run this

Start by asking what brought them here, then route to the right play. If they're not sure, run **Play 1 (the diagnostic)** first — it tells them which of the others they need.

> **What do you need right now?**
> 1. **I'm not sure where I stand** → run the Promotion Gap diagnostic
> 2. **I keep doing the work and someone else gets the credit** → the Credit Moves
> 3. **I want to stay visible week to week without it being a whole project** → the Weekly Visibility System
> 4. **I have a 1:1 coming and I want my boss ready to advocate for me** → the Champion Brief
> 5. **I got overlooked and I need to change how leadership sees me** → the Strategic Assignment Playbook

Run one play fully. Then offer the others — most people need more than one, but not all at once. Pick the one with the least friction and start there.

## Play 1 — The Promotion Gap Diagnostic

Ten questions. Ask them conversationally, in their four groups. For each, the person answers **Yes (2) · Somewhat (1) · No (0)**. Tell them: answer honestly — this is for you, not your manager.

**Impact Reporting**
1. In my last status update or 1:1, I reported outcomes — what was saved, generated, or enabled — not just effort or activity.
2. I can name three specific impact statements from the last 90 days that I've communicated upward.

**Upward Visibility**
3. My boss's boss knows what I've delivered this quarter — not just what my direct manager knows.
4. I have at least one touchpoint per quarter with a leader above my direct manager that isn't a group meeting.

**Sponsorship**
5. I have an explicit sponsor — someone who advocates for me in rooms I'm not in — not just a mentor who gives advice.
6. If my organization held a calibration session today, someone in that room would have my name top of mind.

**Narrative Awareness**
7. I know my current readiness rating — or equivalent — and what specific gap is holding back my promotion.
8. I actively manage my narrative. I don't rely on my work to speak for itself.
9. When I deliver something significant, I make sure the right stakeholders hear about it — not just my direct manager.
10. I can name the specific reason I haven't reached the next level yet — and it isn't "politics" or "timing."

### Score it (out of 20)

| Total | Tier | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 0–7 | **The Invisible Performer** | You're delivering to the technical system. The narrative system has almost no record of you. The calibration room doesn't know what you've built — and the people who decide your next level can't champion what they can't recall. This isn't about the quality of your work. It's about where it lands. |
| 8–13 | **Awareness Gap** | You see the game above your head — you just haven't built consistent habits around it yet. You're writing to the narrative system occasionally, but inconsistency reads as unreliable at the leadership level. The gap is real but closeable. |
| 14–17 | **In the Narrative** | You're playing both systems. The right people have some visibility into your value, and you're managing your narrative with intent. Keep building the habits that close the rest of the gap. |
| 18–20 | **Promotion Ready** | The gap is narrow. The people who decide your next level can see you, know what you've built, and have advocates in the room. At this stage, promotion is an execution question — not a visibility one. Make the ask explicit and the timing right. |

### Name the weakest category

Find the lowest-scoring of the four groups and deliver the matching read:

- **Impact Reporting** — *You're reporting effort, not impact.* The narrative system records outcomes, not hours. "I worked on the migration for six weeks" doesn't survive the calibration room. "The migration cut incident resolution time 40% and freed eight engineering hours a week" does. Same work. Different frame.
- **Upward Visibility** — *You're invisible above your direct manager.* Your manager knows your value. The people deciding your promotion may not. If your boss's boss couldn't describe your last three wins in one sentence, your name is at risk in any calibration session. Visibility above your chain isn't political — it's structural.
- **Sponsorship** — *You have advisors, not advocates.* Mentors help you get better. Sponsors say your name when you're not in the room. If nobody in leadership is actively carrying your name into decisions, you're depending on a system that doesn't default to remembering you.
- **Narrative Awareness** — *Your work is speaking for itself, and it's not enough.* The calibration room doesn't reward the best performer. It rewards the most memorable one. "My work should speak for itself" is the most common reason technically excellent people lose to weaker output with a stronger narrative.

Then point them to the play their weakest category calls for: Impact Reporting / Narrative → Champion Brief or Weekly System; Upward Visibility → Strategic Assignment; Sponsorship → Credit Moves (Move 04).

## Play 2 — The Credit Moves

Credit doesn't travel with the work. It travels with relationships — and with how clearly your name is attached to what you built. Run these before your next major delivery. Don't do all four — pick the lowest-friction one and start.

**Move 01 — The Pre-Brief.** Brief the decision-maker directly *before* the presentation — not after, not through a chain. One conversation. You become the source they already heard from, not the person they heard about.
- *Say:* "Here's what you need to know before you hear about this from anyone else."
- *When:* 24–48 hours before the review. *Who:* your skip-level or the decision-maker receiving the work.

**Move 02 — The Paper Trail That Travels.** Make your name structurally inseparable from the deliverable. When someone lifts it into a deck, your name goes with it — or they have to actively remove it. Different calculation than hoping they remember.
- *Apply to:* document titles, file names, email subjects, summary headers, slide footers. Not "Q2 Migration Plan" — "[Your Name]'s Q2 Migration Plan."

**Move 03 — The Visibility Ritual.** One sentence to your skip-level after every significant delivery. Not a status report. A signal. Builds a running picture of who's handling what — without ever asking for credit.
- *Say:* "Team delivered the firewall migration early. Zero issues — great work from everyone."
- *Format:* email or Slack, never more than three sentences. **This one takes 30 seconds. If you closed something this week and didn't send it — send it tonight.**

**Move 04 — The Sponsorship Calibration.** Name the person who says your name in rooms you're not in. A mentor gives advice. A sponsor advocates when something's on the table.
- *Ask yourself:* who's in the room when your name comes up positively at the VP level or above? If you can't name them, you don't have one yet.
- *Assignment:* pick one leader two levels up. In the next 30 days, find one genuine way to be useful to them.

## Play 3 — The Weekly Visibility System

Five minutes every Monday. You're not building a personal brand — you're maintaining an accurate record of yourself in the minds of the people who decide what happens next.

**The five presence signals** (which are you actively maintaining?)
1. **Snapshot Management** — I know my skip-level's current mental model of me.
2. **Pre-brief Positioning** — I'm in conversations before they become calendar invites.
3. **Skip-level Visibility** — I've sent a direct update one level above my manager in the last 90 days.
4. **Sponsor Coverage** — Someone in the key rooms knows my full picture, not just my label.
5. **Hybrid Entry Points** — I'm present at the informal touchpoints where real decisions happen.

**The Monday audit** (yes or no, no hedging)
1. Can my skip-level describe my current impact in two sentences right now?
2. Did my work reach anyone above my direct manager last week?
3. Am I present at any informal touchpoint this week where real decisions happen?
4. Does my sponsor know what I'm currently working on?
5. If my name came up in a room today, who would tell my full story?

**The quarterly snapshot** — one direct skip-level update per quarter. Three sentences, not a status report:
> "Quick update: [what I delivered and what it enabled]. Currently focused on [current initiative and why it matters to the org]. One thing to have on your radar: [what I need you aware of]."

**The 2-minute test:** *"They would describe my current impact as ___________."* If you hesitate, that's your visibility gap. The work is current. The reputation isn't.

## Play 4 — The Champion Brief

Your boss cannot champion what they cannot explain. Help the person fill this out and send it **24 hours before their 1:1** — it reduces their boss's cognitive load so the boss can move things in their direction.

Walk them through three sections. Push for specifics and business outcomes, not activity.

**Section 1 — What I delivered and what it enabled.** 2–3 things completed this quarter, each tied to an outcome the boss can repeat upward.
> *e.g.* "Completed the network segmentation project — cut audit scope 40%, which clears a blocker on the Q3 compliance review."

**Section 2 — What I'm working on and why it matters to the org.** Current focus, connected to an org priority or something the boss's boss cares about. No jargon.
> *e.g.* "Leading the vendor consolidation — cuts SaaS spend ~$200K and simplifies the security surface before the board review."

**Section 3 — The one ask.** One thing. The single ask the boss needs to be ready to support or defend. Specific.
> *e.g.* "When the budget conversation comes up, I need you ready to say why the infrastructure upgrade is non-negotiable for the migration timeline."

One ask. Not three. The constraint is the point.

## Play 5 — The Strategic Assignment Playbook

Leadership doesn't change how they see you because you did good work. They change it because you showed up in a room that mattered — and they noticed. Four moves to reset your narrative.

First, the question to answer: **what's the highest-visibility, highest-friction problem in your org right now that nobody has claimed?** That's the assignment. You don't need to be the most qualified — you need to be the one who raised their hand while everyone else calculated the risk.

**Move 01 — Raise your hand for the room nobody wants.** Perception resets fastest in high-friction situations — the difficult stakeholder, the impossible timeline, the project everyone declined.
- *Say:* "I'll take it." Two words, before someone fills the silence with reasons it can't be done.
- *Look for:* visible risk **and** visible leadership attention, both at once. *Avoid:* high-effort, low-visibility work.

**Move 02 — Perform visibly, not just successfully.** Success no one witnesses doesn't change your story. Mid-assignment — not at kickoff, not at completion — brief a decision-maker directly on what you're navigating. Once is enough.
- *Say:* "Quick picture of where this stands before it hits your desk — here's what we're dealing with and what I'm doing about it."
- *Who:* the person two levels up who'll hear the outcome anyway.

**Move 03 — Name the result in their language.** Translate the outcome into what leadership tracks: risk removed, budget protected, timeline held. Give them the sentence they'll repeat in the next exec meeting.
- *Say:* "Delivered on time, under budget, client signed the extension. Zero escalations."
- *Test:* could a VP repeat it in a meeting without looking it up? If not, simplify. One email, three sentences, no attachments.

**Move 04 — Lock in the new story within 30 days.** One assignment shifts perception; it doesn't reset it permanently. The window is 30 days. Do one more visible thing in it — a brief, a small win, a useful question in the right meeting — and the new story sticks.
- *Say (a follow-up two weeks later):* "Thought you'd want to know — the fix is holding. No issues since launch."
- One action. Small is fine. Silence is the only wrong answer. Put the deadline on your calendar now.
