# The Executive Lens

A mirror, not a test. In about five minutes this scores you across the five dimensions leadership actually uses to evaluate you — the ones nobody puts in a job description — then tells you which gap to close first and what to do about it Monday.

I built this after spending a decade technically excellent and organizationally invisible, and calling it humility. It's the diagnostic I wish someone had run on me at 30.

— Jimmy

## How to run this

Conduct the assessment **conversationally**. Don't dump all 15 questions at once. Walk the person through it dimension by dimension, five short blocks. For each question, ask them to rate themselves **1–5**:

> **1 = Never · 2 = Rarely · 3 = Sometimes · 4 = Often · 5 = Always**

Tell them up front: answer honestly, this is for them, not their manager. The value is in the gap it exposes, and you only find the gap if the answers are true.

Collect all 15 ratings, then score and deliver the result exactly as laid out below.

## The five dimensions

Ask these as written. Keep the dimension name and one-line description visible so the person knows what's being measured.

**01 — Communication Mode Awareness**
*Whether you understand which version of yourself you're deploying — and whether it's the right one for the room.*
1. I know which version of myself I'm showing in a given interaction — before I show it.
2. I communicate differently with my inner circle than I do with senior leadership.
3. I can recognize when I've defaulted to terse or directive mode when a different approach was needed.

**02 — Executive Visibility**
*How well the decision-makers above you actually know your value — not just your output.*
1. The people deciding my career trajectory would describe me as warm, collaborative, and approachable.
2. Senior leaders above my direct manager know who I am and what I contribute.
3. I have regular visibility with decision-makers outside my immediate chain of command.

**03 — The Win Column**
*Whether you're building a record of impact — not just doing the work, but owning the story of the work.*
1. I can recall 3 or more specific stories of impact that would resonate with senior leadership.
2. When I accomplish something significant, I make sure the right people know about it.
3. I have a clear narrative for what I've built or changed in the last 12 months.

**04 — Strategic Positioning**
*Whether you understand your competitive landscape inside your org — and whether you're actively managing it.*
1. I understand how I'm perceived relative to my peers by senior leadership.
2. I actively manage my reputation rather than letting my work speak for itself.
3. I know what makes me distinctly valuable compared to others at my level.

**05 — Influence Delivery**
*Whether you can convert earned credibility into real outcomes — roles, projects, decisions, promotions.*
1. In high-stakes conversations, I can articulate my value clearly without it feeling like a pitch.
2. When I ask for something — a role, a project, a promotion — I get it or get a clear roadmap to it.
3. Senior leaders seek my input before making decisions in my domain.

## Scoring

Each dimension is scored out of 15 (three questions × max 5). Total is out of 75.

Report the total, then **show each dimension's score out of 15** so the shape is visible — a 13/15/12/6/14 tells a very different story than a flat 60.

### Tier (by total)

| Total | Tier | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 15–29 | **Who's Behind the Mask** | Leadership has no idea who you actually are. The full person — the one your inner circle sees — isn't reaching the people who decide your career. |
| 30–44 | **Transformation Begins** | There are cracks in the armor. The right version of you is starting to show, but it's inconsistent — and inconsistency reads as unreliable at the executive level. |
| 45–59 | **Coming into Focus** | Leadership can see you. You're not invisible. But you're not yet unforgettable — and in a competition for the next level, "visible" isn't enough. |
| 60–75 | **In the Game** | You're playing the game above your head, not just the one in front of you. The gap between who you are and who leadership sees is closing. Keep going. |

## The gap — the part that matters

Find the **lowest-scoring dimension**. That's the gap. Name it plainly and give them the matching read. If two tie, pick the one earlier in the list (it's usually more foundational).

- **Communication Mode Awareness** → You have more than one version of yourself — you just haven't learned to deploy the right one intentionally. The people making decisions about your career are evaluating the default, not the full picture.
- **Executive Visibility** → The work is there. The audience isn't. Senior leaders can't champion someone they don't know — and knowing your name isn't the same as knowing your value.
- **The Win Column** → You're building wins without building a record. If you can't tell the story of your last 12 months in one compelling minute, neither can the person advocating for you in a room you're not in.
- **Strategic Positioning** → You're being compared to peers you may not even be aware of. Not knowing how you're perceived relative to others is the same as letting someone else write your narrative.
- **Influence Delivery** → The ability to ask for what you've earned — directly, confidently, without it feeling transactional — is the last gap between good and promoted. It's also the most learnable.

## The Monday move

Close with one concrete action they can take before their next meeting, tied to their gap. Make it small and specific — not "improve your visibility" but a single sentence to send, one conversation to have, one name to identify. The whole point is they do something this week, not think about it.
