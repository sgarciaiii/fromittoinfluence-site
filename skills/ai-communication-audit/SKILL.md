# The AI Communication Audit

You've sent the message. Now you're waiting — wondering if it landed the way you meant, or if it made you sound smaller than you are. This tells you before you hit send next time.

This isn't a self-assessment. It runs on your *actual* writing and reports how it reads to people who don't know you yet — the rooms that decide your promotion, projects, and budget.

— Jimmy

## Step 0 — Get the material

Ask the person to paste in a sample of their **real** sent messages — emails, Teams/Slack chats, whatever they actually write. Aim for **15–30 messages from the last 30–60 days**. More is better; the patterns sharpen with volume.

If they're running this in a tool connected to their inbox (Perplexity with Outlook/Teams, or Claude with the relevant connector), tell them to let it pull the last 30–60 days directly.

One rule, say it plainly: **read the first report straight through without defending it.** The resistance is data.

## The analysis

Run all seven steps. Base everything **only** on the messages provided — never stereotypes, never assumptions about the person beyond what the text shows.

> **STEP 1 — Tonal Tension Scan.** Identify the dominant emotional tones in the writing (calm, urgent, warm, detached, playful, frustrated, optimistic, skeptical, etc.). Note any *tension* between tones — encouraging and impatient at once, or confident and anxious. Briefly describe how that tension likely feels to the reader.
>
> **STEP 2 — Three-Word Tonal Label.** Create a concise three-word phrase capturing this person's overall "tonal tension." Format: `Three-word tonal label: Word1 – Word2 – Word3`. Each word specific and grounded in the actual messages. This is their communication fingerprint — not who they are, but who their writing says they are to someone who doesn't know them yet. The tension between the words is the finding.
>
> **STEP 3 — Tone & Soft-Skill Assessment.** Score each dimension **1–10** with 1–2 honest sentences:
> - Clarity of communication
> - Emotional awareness / empathy
> - Assertiveness vs. passivity
> - Collaboration and openness to input
> - Conflict handling (directness, defensiveness, avoidance)
> - Professional warmth (approachability, respect, courtesy)
>
> **STEP 4 — Personality & Character Signals.** Infer likely traits. Call out **both** strength signals and risk signals. Speak plainly to a high performer who wants the truth, not flattery. If something reads as impatient, controlling, vague, or defensive — say so, and explain the impact. Every risk signal has a corresponding strength signal: the same directness that makes someone a credible operator can read as dismissive. Name both.
>
> **STEP 5 — Impact on Others.** Best case and worst case. Flag any patterns of active or passive incivility — the kind the sender rarely intends and the recipient feels every time.
>
> **STEP 6 — Concrete Adjustments.** 3–5 specific, *behavioral* micro-adjustments. Not generic advice — specific to what you observed in their messages.
>
> **STEP 7 — Scenario Simulations.** Simulate how this person writes in three situations:
> 1. Delivering tough feedback to a high performer
> 2. Pushing back on an unrealistic request from above
> 3. Admitting a mistake that impacted a key project
>
> For each, write **Version A** (their natural tone, extrapolated from the data) and **Version B** (preserves their personality, reduces relationship risk). One line after each pair: what changed and why. Version B is not a softer version of them — it's the same directness with the relational architecture that makes it land. Point out that what changed is almost never the content.

## How to read the scores

- **7–10 (strengths):** Genuine. Don't change these — amplify them.
- **5–6 (the gap zone):** The instinct is there, the execution isn't consistent. Highest-leverage place to improve.
- **1–4 (structural):** Patterns actively costing them. Direct attention here first.

The most important score to focus on first is **the one that surprises them.**

## Going deeper (offer these follow-ups)

The first report is a diagnosis. These turn it into a strategy. Offer them after the base report:

1. *"Expand the dataset to 100 messages and see if anything changes."*
2. *"One of my closest colleagues has called me [nickname] for years. Do you see it in the data?"* — context the raw text can't carry.
3. *"That same person has followed me across three roles. Does that change the read?"* — loyalty signals. This is where the nuance lives.

When the AI seems wrong, ask them: has someone in your life said something similar? If yes, the resistance is the finding.
